The concrete values in this problem have been replaced by placeholders: Person1, Person2, ... for the friends, L1, L2, ... for locations, T1, T2, ... for clock times, D1, D2, ... for minimum meeting lengths, and a table TRAVEL of travel times. Placeholder numbers carry no meaning; in particular, T1, T2, ... are not in chronological order. The values are the ones of an ordinary planning problem stated as written: every window "from Ta to Tb" ends after it starts, and all times lie within one day.

You are not told the values, and you do not need them: when your program runs, two dicts are already defined. VALUES contains exactly one entry per placeholder, mapping its name (a string such as "T1") to its value, and nothing else: no lists of friends, windows or constraints. Friends and locations are strings (e.g. "Mission District"), clock times are 12-hour strings with no space before AM/PM (e.g. "9:00AM" or "2:45PM"), and D1, D2, ... are integer numbers of minutes. TRAVEL[a][b] is the integer number of minutes to travel from location a to location b, where a and b are location placeholder names (e.g. TRAVEL["L1"]["L2"]). Your program must therefore write out the problem's structure itself, in terms of placeholder names (for example friends = [("Person1", "L2", "T2", "T15", "D2"), ...] and the start location and time), and look up each placeholder's value in VALUES or TRAVEL. It must not define or overwrite VALUES or TRAVEL, and must print the answer with the actual values (the real names, locations and times), not the placeholder names.
