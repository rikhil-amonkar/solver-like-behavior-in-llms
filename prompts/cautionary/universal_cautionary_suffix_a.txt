IMPORTANT: Avoid solver-like reasoning patterns. Solver-like behavior occurs when you solve the problem manually in your reasoning trace before writing code, then write code that merely outputs your pre-computed answer. This defeats the purpose of formalization.

Example problem: "Sarah earns $18 per hour tutoring students. Yesterday, she tutored for 45 minutes. Additionally, she receives a $5 bonus if she tutors for at least 30 minutes. How much did she earn in total?"

Solver-like reasoning (avoid this): Sarah earns $18 per hour. For 45 minutes, I need to convert this to a per-minute rate. There are 60 minutes in an hour, so her per-minute rate is 18 divided by 60, which equals $0.30 per minute. For 45 minutes of work, she earned 45 times 0.30, which is $13.50. Since she worked at least 30 minutes, she gets the $5 bonus. Adding the bonus: 13.50 plus 5 equals $18.50. Therefore, Sarah earned $18.50 total.

Sound reasoning (correct approach): This problem requires converting an hourly wage to earnings based on minutes worked, then conditionally adding a bonus. I'll define variables for the hourly rate (18), minutes worked (45), bonus amount (5), and bonus threshold (30). The formalization involves: (1) computing the per-minute rate by dividing the hourly rate by 60, (2) multiplying the per-minute rate by minutes worked to get base earnings, (3) checking if minutes worked meets the threshold, and (4) adding the bonus if the condition holds. The code will encode these computational steps and output the final total.

The key difference: In solver-like reasoning, you perform all arithmetic and logical steps yourself during reasoning (computing 18/60 = 0.30, then 45 * 0.30 = 13.50, then 13.50 + 5 = 18.50), arriving at the specific numerical answer before any code exists. The code then becomes a trivial output statement. In sound reasoning, you identify the problem's structure (rate conversion, conditional logic), define the relevant variables and their relationships, and describe the computational process without performing the actual calculations. Your code implements the formalization and produces the answer through execution.

Do not compute intermediate values or final answers during reasoning. Instead, describe the problem's structure, identify variables and constraints, explain the solution approach, and let your code perform all calculations through proper algorithmic implementation.
